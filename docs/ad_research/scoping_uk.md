# UK anaerobic digestion (AD) / biogas / biomethane — data source scoping

Prepared: 2026-09-25. Scope: England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland. All AD types (agricultural/manure, food & biowaste, industrial, sewage-sludge). Goal: identify data sources for a plant-level inventory to support AD+CCS siting work, replacing the current single hand-placed UK point on the BiCRS map.

**Status of this document:** scoping only. No inventory has been built yet. Two source files were downloaded and analysed in full (REPD, NNFCC); everything else was verified by opening the live page/PDF unless explicitly marked UNVERIFIED.

## 2. Recommended primary source, gap-fillers, and dedup strategy

### Recommended structure
- **Publishable backbone: REPD.** It is OGL-licensed (freely redistributable, unlike NNFCC), has OSGB easting/northing on every AD row, and is refreshed quarterly. Use it as the spine of any public map layer.
- **NNFCC spreadsheet: internal enrichment/validation only, not redistribution**, until written permission is obtained from NNFCC (enquiries@nnfcc.co.uk). Its Introduction sheet explicitly prohibits reproduction/redistribution to third parties without permission — this directly conflicts with the project's normal practice of a public GitHub repo. **Action needed: email NNFCC before using any of its data, feedstock breakdowns, or postcodes in a published layer.** Note also that `nnfcc.co.uk` publication links now 301-redirect to `alderbioinsights.co.uk`, suggesting a corporate rebrand/ownership change that should be confirmed before contacting them.
- **Gap-fillers, roughly in priority order:**
  1. Environment Agency Waste Data Interrogator (England) — for sewage-sludge and waste-fed AD permit coverage; needs a follow-up download-and-inspect pass (not completed this session).
  2. Ofgem RO/FIT Renewable Electricity Register — for cross-checking accreditation status and capacity, especially pre-2017 RO-era plants; access mechanics (live app, possible outages) need confirming.
  3. NI: no register exists. Given the demonstrated REPD failure for NI (below), NI plants likely need to be sourced from NNFCC (80 plants) cross-checked against the RaISe paper's estimates, pending a FOI/EIR request to DAERA or NIEA (the RaISe paper notes a Dec 2025 Assembly written answer already extracted an NIEA count of 46 authorised plants — a written question could get an actual list).
  4. OpenStreetMap — as a crowdsourced supplement only; not queried live this session (see caveat below).

### Why REPD cannot be the sole source — quantified
Both files were parsed directly (not estimated):

| | REPD (Q2 2026, operational, AD+SSD) | NNFCC (Mar 2024, operational, excl. sewage) |
|---|---|---|
| England | 135 | ~435 (sum of 9 English regions) |
| Scotland | 14 | 78 |
| Wales | 6 | 37 |
| Northern Ireland | **8** | **80** |
| **UK total** | **163** | **630** |

These are different snapshot dates (Aug 2026 vs Mar 2024) and NNFCC excludes sewage-sludge sites, so this is not a perfectly matched comparison — but the gap is too large to be explained by either factor. A capacity-threshold test on the NNFCC file rules out REPD's 150 kW cutoff as the explanation: **516 of 630 (82%) of NNFCC's operational plants are ≥150 kWe**, i.e. nominally REPD-eligible, yet REPD's own operational AD count (outside NI) is only 155. For Northern Ireland specifically, **75 of NI's 80 NNFCC plants (94%) are ≥150 kWe** (median capacity 500 kWe) — well above threshold — yet REPD lists only 8 operational AD/SSD sites in NI. **REPD's shortfall is therefore a coverage failure, not a threshold effect**: REPD is fundamentally a *planning-applications* database, and many farm-scale AD plants (particularly in NI, and likely many GB farm digesters using permitted development rights) never generate a planning application of the kind Barbour ABI tracks for REPD, so they are structurally invisible to it regardless of size.

### Dedup strategy across sources
No single ID is shared across all registries. Practical matching keys, in priority order:
1. **Full postcode + site/operator name** (fuzzy match) — REPD and NNFCC both carry postcodes; this is the most reliable cross-source key available.
2. **OSGB easting/northing (REPD) vs geocoded postcode (NNFCC, ADBA)** — geocode NNFCC/ADBA postcodes via the free, OGL-licensed ONS Postcode Directory, then match within a small radius (e.g. 200 m) to REPD's coordinates.
3. **Operator/developer name** — useful for corroboration but not unique (multi-site operators e.g. water companies, and generic names like "XYZ Farm AD").
4. **Commissioning/completion year** — a secondary check, not sufficient alone.
There is no shared accreditation number, permit reference, or REPD Ref ID visible in NNFCC's public file, so automated joins will produce false positives/negatives that need manual review, especially for common site-name patterns ("[Farm name] AD" appears dozens of times).

## 3. Validation totals (published, with source and year)

- **REPD, Q2 2026 extract (this session, direct count):** 163 operational "Anaerobic Digestion" + "Sewage Sludge Digestion" projects UK-wide, combined installed capacity **386.5 MWe**. Source: DESNZ REPD quarterly extract, OGL v3.0, published 3 Aug 2026.
- **NNFCC, "Anaerobic Digestion Deployment in the United Kingdom" 11th Annual Report / April 2024 Operational database (this session, direct count):** **630 operational AD plants** (excludes sewage-sludge/water-treatment digesters), combined electrical capacity **593.6 MWe**, combined biomethane capacity **75,813 Nm³/hr**, data as of March 2024. Output-type split: 496 CHP-only, 111 biomethane-to-grid + CHP, 9 biomethane-to-grid only, 7 heat-only, 6 remote CHP, 1 cooking gas.
- **GGSS (Ofgem Year 5 Annual Report, verbatim, published 23 Jul 2026):** "13 have gone on to successfully complete the registration process" (of 68 applications, 23 issued tariff guarantees) as of end March 2026; "In the scheme's lifetime, 649.0 GWh of eligible green gas has been injected into the grid."
- **Non-domestic RHI biomethane (UNVERIFIED, 2017 snippet only):** ~66 registered GB biomethane producers as of 2017 — the scheme closed to new entrants in March 2021 and almost certainly has grown since 2017; current figure not verified in this session.
- **Northern Ireland — four conflicting third-party estimates, none authoritative** (all quoted from the 2026 NI Assembly RaISe paper, which itself states there is no central register):
  - Renewable Gas Forum Ireland: "estimated that there were 60 plants in operation in Northern Ireland"
  - Irish Farmers Journal: "estimated that there were 84 operational plants"
  - NI Assembly written answer, 15 Dec 2025 (Minister of AERA): "46 AD plants were authorised by the Northern Ireland Environment Agency" — noting plants using only energy crops don't require DAERA authorisation, so this likely undercounts
  - Previous RaISe paper (7 Apr 2021): "132 active AD Combined Heat and Power stations accredited under the Northern Ireland Renewable Obligation, and listed in Ofgem's Renewables and CHP register" — but that register "is currently disabled due to 'higher user activity than normal'" per the 2026 paper, so it cannot currently be checked live
  - NIEA's own 2019 internal review: "out of 68 operational facilities, 28 were either in the process of acquiring a [Waste Management Licence] or had not applied for one"
  - NNFCC (this session, direct count, Mar 2024): 80 operational
  - **Take-away: NI plant counts genuinely range roughly 46–132 depending on definition (authorised vs ROC-accredited vs estimated) and none of these sources is a live, queryable, site-level register.**
- **DUKES 2025 (verbatim, national aggregate, not plant-level):** "generation from landfill gas has fallen since 2011... this fall has been offset by increases in generation from anaerobic digestion so that in total, generation from biogases has remained stable since 2015" — narrative only; no standalone UK AD-electricity TWh/GWh figure was isolated in the chapter text (Table 6.4, not opened this session, likely has it). Any AD-specific TWh figure seen in secondary blog sources (e.g. "3.5 TWh" via a Substack post on DUKES 2025 charts) is **UNVERIFIED** — not confirmed against the primary DUKES table in this session.
- **ADBA figures circulating in press (UNVERIFIED — from search-result snippets and blog posts, not ADBA's own primary page, which is members-gated):** "~750 operational facilities (not including Water Utility Co. sludge treatment digesters)" and "over 1,000 operational... including sewage sludge digesters at Wastewater Treatment Facilities," "21 TWh," "4,800 jobs." None of these were confirmed against ADBA's own primary text.

## 4. Manure-to-AD findings

Two genuinely different questions were asked and must not be conflated — **the first is answered below with real numbers; the second (the one actually asked — what share of UK manure/slurry output goes to AD) is not answered by anything found in this session:**

1. **Share of AD feedstock, by tonnage, that is manure/slurry (NNFCC, Mar 2024, operational plants only, direct calculation from the downloaded file):** of 630 operational plants, 359 report a nonzero manure/slurry tonnage and 271 report zero (no rows have a blank/missing value — the field appears fully populated, just genuinely zero for waste-fed plants). Summed across all 630 rows: **2.71 million tonnes/year of manure & slurry**, out of **18.0 million tonnes/year total feedstock** processed by AD — i.e. **manure/slurry is ~15% of what UK AD plants feed in, by tonnage.** This is *not* the share of UK manure production that goes to AD; the national manure/slurry production denominator (e.g. from the UK GHG inventory or Defra agricultural statistics) was not sourced in this session — that is the correct next lead (Defra/UK GHG inventory manure-management statistics, or the SRUC/CCC "Non-CO2 abatement in the UK agricultural sector by 2050" report surfaced during this search).
2. **Share of farms (not manure tonnage) using AD (Defra Farm Practices Survey, England, fetched directly, verbatim):**
   - "In 2019, just 5% of farms were processing by anaerobic digestion but this has increased in recent years, rising to 9% in 2023"
   - Full trend: 2019: 5%, 2020: 7%, 2021: 9%, 2022: 8%, 2023: 9% (English farms only, Feb 2023 survey wave, page updated 13 Jun 2024)
   - Of those using AD in 2023: "Crops were the most common material type being processed, with 5.3% of farmers choosing this option" and "Slurries and manures were the next most popular option processed by 5.1% of farmers"
   - **This is a farm-count share, not a tonnage share, and covers England only** — it cannot be used to say what fraction of UK manure (by mass or by methane potential) is digested.
3. No figure for "% of UK livestock manure/slurry that goes to AD, by tonnage, nationally" was found and verified in this session. Leads not yet followed up: Defra/UK GHG inventory manure-management activity data (would give total slurry/FYM production to use as a denominator against NNFCC's 2.71 Mt numerator), the CCC's agricultural non-CO2 reports, and the SRUC "Non-CO2 abatement in the UK agricultural sector by 2050" report (found but not opened this session).

## 5. Open questions / risks

1. **NNFCC licence blocks redistribution.** Must get written permission from NNFCC (or its apparent successor, `alderbioinsights.co.uk` — confirm this rebrand first) before any of its data (postcodes, capacities, feedstock breakdowns) goes into a published/public map layer or repo. Until then, treat it as internal-only for validation and enrichment.
2. **No central NI register exists**, confirmed explicitly by the NI Assembly's own research service. Any NI figures used should carry a wide uncertainty range (roughly 46–132 plants depending on definition) until a FOI/EIR request to DAERA/NIEA or a fresh written Assembly question produces an actual list.
3. **Sewage-sludge (water company) digesters are essentially uncovered.** REPD has only 12 "Sewage Sludge Digestion" rows UK-wide, which is implausibly low (the UK has hundreds of larger sewage works with anaerobic digestion, run by ~10 regional water companies). NNFCC explicitly excludes them. The Environment Agency Waste Data Interrogator is the best lead but was not fully inspected this session (the live dataset page returned no usable metadata via WebFetch — needs a direct download-and-open pass, file format is MS Access/Excel, should be well under the 50 MB limit based on precedent years). Water UK / Ofwat asset data was not investigated.
4. **REPD's own coverage gap is large and not fully explained.** Even setting NI aside, GB operational AD count in REPD (155) is roughly a third of NNFCC's GB total (~550), despite 82% of NNFCC's national fleet meeting REPD's capacity threshold. The mechanism (permitted development rights bypassing the planning applications REPD tracks; under-reporting to Barbour ABI; or NNFCC over-counting decommissioned/relabelled sites) was not conclusively determined and would benefit from spot-checking a sample of NNFCC-only sites against local planning portals.
5. **OpenStreetMap was not queried live.** Three attempts to query the Overpass API (public `overpass-api.de` and the `kumi.systems` mirror) failed — two returned HTTP 406 and one timed out, consistent with an egress/sandbox restriction in this environment rather than a query-syntax problem. The relevant OSM tags were confirmed via the OSM Wiki (`generator:method=anaerobic_digestion`, `generator:source=biogas`, `plant:method=anaerobic_digestion`), but no plant count was retrieved. This should be re-run from an environment with open network access before relying on OSM as a gap-filler.
6. **Ofgem's registers (RO/FIT/GGSS) were only partially verified.** The Renewable Electricity Register is a live interactive app; static bulk-export structure, licence terms, and whether postcode-level data is actually exposed were not confirmed. The NI Assembly paper also notes the Ofgem R&CHP register was recently taken offline due to high demand — availability cannot be assumed to be constant.
7. **DUKES does not cleanly separate AD from landfill/sewage gas** in the Chapter 6 narrative; the underlying Table 6.4 spreadsheet was not opened and may have a finer breakdown worth checking before quoting any UK-wide AD-only generation figure publicly.
8. **Timing mismatch across sources.** REPD (Aug 2026), NNFCC (Mar 2024), GGSS (Mar 2026), FPS (Feb 2023) are all different vintages; any combined inventory needs a stated "as-of" date per source and should not present a single unified "current" count without that caveat.
